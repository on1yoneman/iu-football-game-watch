Game Preview · Washington

Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · original integrated tactical preview
INDIANA (9–1, 6–1 BIG TEN) AT WASHINGTON (6–4, 3–4) · NOV. 21 · 3:30 PM ET · PEACOCK · SEATTLE

Make Washington win the hard yards

Indiana arrives in Seattle with a new quarterback, a reshuffled offensive line and a defense that has kept even strong opponents in check. Washington has a capable vertical passer and a home crowd, but a weak conventional run game. The Hoosiers' best path is to win first down, protect Grant Wilson from obvious passing downs, and force Demond Williams Jr. to drive the field against a rotating front rather than create explosive plays off broken contain.

Washington averages 267.4 passing yards but only 93.8 rushing yards per game, while Indiana allows 77.8 on the ground. Those numbers invite Indiana to squeeze interior gaps and ask Williams to beat disguised coverage on third-and-long. That is not permission for reckless blitzing. Against Michigan State, Williams threw for 288 yards and two touchdowns, both to Chris Lawson. Indiana should use Tobi Osunsanmi and Stephen Daley to pressure without surrendering the outside escape lane, then mix simulated pressure with late safety rotations. The secondary's tackling matters: A.J. Harris is Doubtful on the initial report, and Washington can isolate Lawson against the replacement corner or stress underneath zones with crossing routes. Jamari Sharpe, Jaylen Bell and the safety rotation will need sound communication, while Jacob Savage's 13 tackles against USC show the linebacker depth can handle heavy snaps.

Indiana's offense is not the same unit that began October. Grant Wilson passed for 305 yards and two touchdowns against USC but also threw an interception and put several other throws at risk. His deep ball gives Nick Marsh, Charlie Becker and Shazz Preston ways to punish Washington's single-high coverage. The counter is ball security: use quick rhythm throws, movement pockets and the tight ends to make Washington defend width before calling long-developing routes. Joe Brunner is officially Out, and Austin Leibfried handled all 64 snaps at right guard against USC; keep protection calls simple and use Trevor Gibbs or Brock Schott as a chipper when Washington presents edge pressure. If questionable edge defenders Logan George and Devin Hyde are limited, Indiana can lean more heavily on outside-zone and play-action sequences.

Turbo Richard remains Out, so Lee Beebe Jr. and Khobie Martin carry the rushing load. Martin gained 82 yards on 12 carries against USC; Beebe adds dependable pass protection. Their different strengths make third-down substitution revealing. Washington's Trey Cooley is Doubtful, while receivers Dezmen Roebuck and Jordan Clay are Questionable. If Roebuck cannot go, the Huskies become more Lawson-centric, giving Indiana a clearer bracket target. If he plays, Indiana must protect the intermediate middle and prevent a quick-game chain of completions from neutralizing the rush. Washington's defensive backs can create short fields, so the Hoosiers should favor patient possessions over forcing contested sideline shots.

The game flow is likely to turn on field position. Washington has three one-score losses and has defended well enough to keep opponents close, but Indiana's third-down defense, low rushing allowance and ability to rotate defensive linemen favor the visitors. Washington's best counter is a fast passing start that forces IU out of its conservative offensive script. Indiana's best counter is to run into light boxes, make the Huskies tackle, and save the shot plays for second-and-short. The official first report makes the Harris/Lawson matchup more precarious but does not erase Indiana's advantage in the trenches.
