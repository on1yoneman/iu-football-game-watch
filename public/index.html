Game Preview · USC

November 3, 2026 · bye-week tactical scouting edition
INDIANA (8–1, 5–1 BIG TEN) VS USC (7–2, 4–2) · NOV. 14 · 3:30 PM ET · CBS · BLOOMINGTON

The space between pressure and explosive plays: Indiana's two-week problem against USC

Indiana and USC arrive at the same bye from opposite directions. The Hoosiers have won two consecutive overtime games by leaning on their defense and late execution. The Trojans just upset Ohio State 30–18 with explosive skill-position plays after scoring only seven points at Wisconsin. This is a matchup between a defensive front that can wreck a drive and an offense that can score before the front has time to establish control. The central question is whether Indiana can turn its pressure into hurried, contained throws without surrendering yards after the catch.

USC's first two snaps against Ohio State captured the danger. Freshman Trent Mosley took a short completion behind the line of scrimmage 69 yards for a touchdown, then finished with five catches for 101 yards. Tight end Mark Bowman later turned a catch into a 70-yard gain. These are documented plays in USC's Oct. 31 game account, not claims of independent All-22 review. Lincoln Riley will use wide alignments, motion and quick access throws to make Indiana's edge rushers hesitate before attacking. A corner who retreats too quickly gives up the screen; a safety who triggers downhill too aggressively risks a seam or wheel behind him. Indiana should show two-high structure before the snap, rotate late when appropriate, and demand that its outside defenders force short throws back inside to pursuit. The first tackler must finish.

USC quarterback Jayden Maiava has a productive downfield arm and USC entered its bye with seven wins, but the contrast between the Wisconsin loss and Ohio State upset shows why the first-down battle matters. Against Ohio State, USC scored 30 on only 338 yards and 50 plays; the Buckeyes gained 464 yards on 81 plays but repeatedly settled for field goals. USC's short fields and explosive gains were decisive. Against Wisconsin, the Trojans managed seven points. Indiana's preferred plan is to make Maiava operate on third-and-long against Tobi Osunsanmi, Stephen Daley and interior pressure from Tyrique Tucker, while using a disciplined four-man rush to keep coverage numbers behind the ball. The risk in frequent all-out pressure is letting Mosley or Bowman beat isolated coverage in space. USC–Ohio State official play and team statistics · USC results.

Indiana's own offensive questions are harder to resolve with a single schematic tweak. Josh Hoover completed eight of 19 passes for 119 yards in the first half against Minnesota before Grant Wilson replaced him; Wilson went nine of 17 for 156 and hit Charlie Becker for the 25-yard overtime winner. The Daily Hoosier's snap and PFF review finds Wilson faced six pressures to Hoover's four yet produced three high-difficulty throws, all to Becker. That suggests the answer is not simply better pass protection. Wilson's ability to extend and deliver deeper throws changes the coverage math. But the staff must evaluate Hoover's health before assuming a permanent quarterback change. Both players should be prepared for USC, and the early plan should give either quarterback defined first reads with an outlet against simulated pressure.

Indiana gained 419 yards against Minnesota but converted only two of 13 third downs and missed or mishandled multiple field goals. Evan Alvarez's data study at Hoosier Huddle identifies third-and-medium as a special problem: Indiana has been unusually predictable in passing on those downs, despite a strong overall net-yards-per-play profile. The interpretation matters. This is not an offense incapable of moving the ball; it is an offense that has struggled to finish drives and sustain its menu when defenses load the box. Against USC, Mike Shanahan should build a first-down run game that uses Lee Beebe Jr.'s contact balance and the backs' perimeter threats, then pair inside-zone looks with play-action, RPO slants and tight-end seams. Brock Schott led Indiana tight ends in Minnesota snaps and could be a useful underneath outlet when USC's linebackers chase the run.

USC's defense also merits more respect after the Ohio State result. It kept the Buckeyes without a touchdown for nearly 57 minutes despite surrendering substantial yardage. But Ohio State was using a freshman quarterback in place of injured Julian Sayin, a meaningful limitation on that comparison. Indiana should test USC's coverage discipline with shifts and bunch releases rather than infer from the final score that the Trojans have suddenly become an elite defense. Charlie Becker is the matchup-defining receiver because his route strength and contested catches give either Indiana quarterback a reliable answer against tight coverage. Nick Marsh's ability to threaten vertically can prevent USC from compressing the field around Becker.

Defensively, the Minnesota game offered evidence Indiana can survive a missing star. TJ Inman's Hoosier Huddle reporting documents Jacob Savage's emergency start when Rolijah Hardy was hurt during warmups. The Daily Hoosier's defensive participation and PFF review shows Savage played all 60 snaps, while Osunsanmi played 54 and Daley 52. Indiana's front generated four sacks and limited Minnesota to 215 yards. USC presents a much more serious yards-after-catch test, however, and A.J. Harris's late injury bears monitoring. Indiana may need to help a replacement corner with cloud coverage, while keeping the opposite safety ready to close on Mosley's quick-game targets.

The likely early game is more measured than USC's explosive Ohio State opening. Both teams have two weeks to build specific answers. Indiana should aim to force long USC drives, avoid giving Maiava free screens against aggressive pressure, and use a run-pass balance that keeps its own offense out of third-and-medium traps. USC's best counter is to score on quick perimeter touches and make Indiana chase points, particularly if Hoover remains limited or Wilson's deep-ball rhythm cools. The matchup could swing sharply on one missed tackle, one turnover or the availability of Hardy and Harris.
